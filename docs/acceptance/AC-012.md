## AC-012-B01 — Delete contact submissions at the retention edge while keeping audit references

- Family: `AC-012`
- Type: `boundary`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `OPS-008`, `OPS-006`, `FND-037`
- Layers: `real-provider-integration`, `operational-recovery`
- Security dependencies: `AC-003`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W audit of the included privileged operations naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, without secrets or personal content; audit reading is separate from administration, collection failures are distinct from absent events, and no tamper-evidence claim is made without a qualified mechanism.

### Preconditions

- The configured contact retention period R and the audit retention policy are recorded. contact-1 is one day younger than R and contact-2 one day older, and audit records exist for both submissions' delivery and access.

### Steps

1. Run the retention job, then read both submissions with contact read permission.
2. Read the audit records that refer to both submissions and the retention job's own record.

### Expected outcome

- contact-1 remains readable and contact-2 is deleted.
- Audit records persist under their own retention with references only and no submission content, and the retention job records its deletion outcome instead of appearing as a period without events.

### Evidence to retain

- Submission ages and states before and after, retention job records and the referencing audit records, as evidence for TM-W-032.

## AC-012-N01 — Keep secrets and personal content out of audit, logs and import reports

- Family: `AC-012`
- Type: `negative`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `OPS-006`, `OPS-007`, `FND-037`, `FND-038`
- Layers: `unit`, `real-provider-integration`
- Security dependencies: `AC-002`, `AC-031`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W audit of the included privileged operations naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, without secrets or personal content; audit reading is separate from administration, collection failures are distinct from absent events, and no tamper-evidence claim is made without a qualified mechanism.

### Preconditions

- Unique sentinel values serve as an Admin password, a one-time code, a recovery code, a session cookie, a contact message body and reply address, and a credential-like assignment in a package text field. A clean package pkg-c and the sentinel-bearing package pkg-s are available, and the secret-finding block on pkg-s is qualified by AC-031.

### Steps

1. Sign in, complete a recovery, submit a contact request, change the contact destination, dry run pkg-s, and dry run, approve and apply pkg-c.
2. Search audit records, logs, traces, dry-run reports and the apply audit report for every sentinel.

### Expected outcome

- No secret sentinel appears anywhere searched, and contact content appears only in the contact store behind its read permission.
- pkg-s's dry-run audit record names the finding's location with the matched value redacted.
- pkg-c's apply report names the approver, plan, digest and per-item outcomes without content values.

### Evidence to retain

- Sentinel search results per store, the pkg-s audit record and the pkg-c apply report, as evidence for TM-W-032 and TM-W-042.

## AC-012-N02 — Deny audit changes and reads outside the separated audit permissions

- Family: `AC-012`
- Type: `negative`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `OPS-007`, `FND-037`, `MOD-005`
- Layers: `isolation`, `api-contract`
- Security dependencies: `AC-003`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W audit of the included privileged operations naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, without secrets or personal content; audit reading is separate from administration, collection failures are distinct from absent events, and no tamper-evidence claim is made without a qualified mechanism.

### Preconditions

- site-admin-1 holds every W content and permission-administration permission but neither audit read nor audit administration; auditor-1 holds audit read only. The release's capability report and any declared audit integrity mechanism are recorded.

### Steps

1. As site-admin-1, read, edit, delete and purge audit records through direct calls.
2. As auditor-1, read records, then attempt to edit and delete them and to change audit retention.
3. If the release declares a qualified integrity mechanism, alter one record directly in PostgreSQL and run its verification procedure; otherwise inspect the capability report's audit-integrity statement.

### Expected outcome

- site-admin-1 can neither read nor change records; auditor-1 reads but cannot change records or retention, and each refusal is itself audited.
- With a declared mechanism, verification reports the altered record; without one, the report makes no tamper-evidence or immutability claim.

### Evidence to retain

- Per-call decisions, record inventories before and after, refusal audit records, and the verification report or capability statement, as evidence for TM-G02-052.

## AC-012-P01 — Record attributable audit entries for each included privileged operation

- Family: `AC-012`
- Type: `positive`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `FND-037`, `OPS-007`, `MOD-004`
- Layers: `real-provider-integration`, `api-contract`
- Security dependencies: `AC-002`, `AC-003`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W audit of the included privileged operations naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, without secrets or personal content; audit reading is separate from administration, collection failures are distinct from absent events, and no tamper-evidence claim is made without a qualified mechanism.

### Preconditions

- The privileged-operation table of the website threat model is the fixture list, from sign-in and factor changes through permission assignment, publication, rollback, media, contact destination, package source, import, alias and documentation-source operations. Each operation has one authorized actor and one actor that lacks the permission.

### Steps

1. Perform each operation once as its authorized actor and attempt it once as the unauthorized actor.
2. Read the resulting records through the audit-read permission and directly from PostgreSQL.

### Expected outcome

- Each success and each refusal has exactly one record naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, committed in the same transaction as any change.
- Factor, contact-destination and package-source records also show the recent-authentication result and the notification sent, and the destination record notes the previous destination's notification.

### Evidence to retain

- The operation-to-record table with both outcomes per operation and the database extract, as evidence for TM-G02-051, TM-W-013, TM-W-033 and TM-W-057.

## AC-012-R01 — Refuse or defer privileged changes while audit persistence fails

- Family: `AC-012`
- Type: `recovery`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `OPS-007`, `FND-037`, `MOD-004`, `FND-038`
- Layers: `real-provider-integration`, `operational-recovery`
- Security dependencies: `AC-003`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W audit of the included privileged operations naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, without secrets or personal content; audit reading is separate from administration, collection failures are distinct from absent events, and no tamper-evidence claim is made without a qualified mechanism.

### Preconditions

- Audit writes can be made to fail at the database for the audit store only. admin-1 is ready to assign a permission, roll back a manifest and change the contact destination.

### Steps

1. Fail audit writes, then attempt each operation.
2. Query the audit view and operational telemetry for the failure window.
3. Clear the fault, retry each operation, and process any deferred change.

### Expected outcome

- Each operation is refused or deferred with a visible reason; no permission, manifest or destination changes without its record.
- The audit view reports a collection failure for the window, distinct from a period without events, and telemetry counts the failed writes.
- After the fault clears, each change commits with its record exactly once, and no deferred change applies twice.

### Evidence to retain

- Fault timeline, operation outcomes, state and record inventories, the collection-failure report and telemetry counters, as evidence for TM-G02-051.

## AC-012-R02 — Account for audit history across a restore

- Family: `AC-012`
- Type: `recovery`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `FND-039`, `OPS-007`, `FND-037`
- Layers: `operational-recovery`, `real-provider-integration`
- Security dependencies: `AC-003`, `AC-031`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W audit of the included privileged operations naming actor, effective subject, tenant, site, resource, action, outcome, time and correlation, without secrets or personal content; audit reading is separate from administration, collection failures are distinct from absent events, and no tamper-evidence claim is made without a qualified mechanism.

### Preconditions

- A coordinated backup was taken before admin-1 changed the contact destination and rebound the package source. Production audit records hold both changes, and an isolated recovery deployment is available.

### Steps

1. Restore the backup into the recovery deployment and read its audit records for the interval after the backup point.
2. Reapply the destination change and the package-source binding there with recent authentication.

### Expected outcome

- The restore is recorded as a privileged event with the backup identity, operator and backup point, and the interval after the backup point is reported as absent from the restored audit set rather than as a period without events.
- Each reapplied change is audited again with its recent-authentication result, and the destination change notifies the previous destination.

### Evidence to retain

- Restore record, audit-gap report, reapplication records and notifications, as evidence for TM-G02-051, TM-W-033 and TM-W-057.
