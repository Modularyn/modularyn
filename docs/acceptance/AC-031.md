## AC-031-B01 — Rotate secrets and keys at their declared lifecycle edges

- Family: `AC-031`
- Type: `boundary`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `OPS-004`, `FND-006`
- Layers: `operational-recovery`, `unit`
- Security dependencies: `AC-002`, `AC-012`

### Profile scope

- `website`: W ASVS Level 2 applicability and production posture for the public, Admin, API, preview, import and contact surfaces: content security policy and framing on the shared origin, HTTPS, release and dependency provenance against the dependency register, secret, key and telemetry handling, hostile package and source input, media scanning and quarantine, host validation and canonical links, contact antispam, public rate limits, backups, and container and network limits. No certification claim and no surface outside W.

### Preconditions

- The email transport credential, the data-protection key ring and the one-time-password seed key each have a current version and a documented rotation procedure. An Admin session and an encrypted seed predate rotation, and the declared retention of the previous data-protection key is recorded.

### Steps

1. Rotate the transport credential and send contact mail immediately before and after the previous credential is revoked.
2. Point the transport at an endpoint that presents an unverifiable certificate and send contact mail.
3. Rotate the key ring and present the earlier session cookie before and after the previous key's declared end.
4. Rotate the seed key and verify a one-time code for an account whose seed was encrypted under the previous key.

### Expected outcome

- Delivery succeeds under each valid credential and is a recorded delivery failure under the revoked one; the unverifiable certificate is a delivery failure with no unencrypted fallback.
- The earlier cookie is accepted until the previous key's declared end and refused after it.
- The earlier seed still verifies through the authorized key store after rotation.

### Evidence to retain

- Rotation records with key and credential versions, delivery states, cookie decisions per edge and code verification results, as evidence for TM-W-034, TM-G02-050 and TM-G02-056.

## AC-031-B03 — Rate-limit public requests and bound rendering cost at the declared edges

- Family: `AC-031`
- Type: `boundary`
- Profiles: `website`
- Requirements: `OPS-018`, `OPS-016`
- Layers: `real-provider-integration`, `api-contract`
- Security dependencies: None.

### Profile scope

- `website`: W ASVS Level 2 applicability and production posture for the public, Admin, API, preview, import and contact surfaces: content security policy and framing on the shared origin, HTTPS, release and dependency provenance against the dependency register, secret, key and telemetry handling, hostile package and source input, media scanning and quarantine, host validation and canonical links, contact antispam, public rate limits, backups, and container and network limits. No certification claim and no surface outside W.

### Preconditions

- The release's declared per-source public request limit L per window, render-time and output-size bounds and small-site workload are recorded before running. Sources src-1 and src-2 are distinct. The active manifest holds a navigation tree and sitemap at the workload's declared maximum size, a fixture page at the output-size bound and a draft one block beyond it.

### Steps

1. From src-1, send L public page requests within one window and then one more, while src-2 sends requests within its own limit throughout.
2. After src-1's window resets, request the sitemap and the page with the largest navigation tree repeatedly, recording render time, output size and whether either is regenerated.
3. Request the fixture page at the output-size bound, then attempt to publish and request the draft beyond it.

### Expected outcome

- The first L requests from src-1 are served and request L plus one is refused with a throttling status; src-2's requests are served throughout.
- Sitemap and navigation responses come from the output precomputed for the active manifest, and repeated requests do not regenerate them.
- The page at the bound renders within the declared render-time bound; the page beyond it is refused, at publication or at render, with a stable error rather than rendered past the bound.

### Evidence to retain

- Per-request source, counter and status; render time and output size per response; and sitemap and navigation generation records, as evidence for TM-W-006.

## AC-031-N06 — Refuse contact submissions that fail antispam or limits without queuing mail

No certification claim and no surface outside W.

### Preconditions

- The contact form has a configured named destination. The declared antispam policy, per-source and per-destination submission limits and windows, field and request size bounds, and outbound delivery cap are recorded before running. Sources src-1 to src-4 are distinct, and the bounded delivery environment captures outbound messages.

### Steps

1. From src-1, submit one request failing each check that the declared antispam policy lists, and one that replays an antispam response already accepted for another submission.
2. From src-2, submit valid requests up to the per-source limit and once more; from src-3, continue toward the same destination until the per-destination limit is reached and once more.
3. Submit a request whose fields exceed the declared size bounds; after the windows elapse, submit from many sources the largest burst of valid requests that the submission limits allow.
4. After the windows elapse again, submit one valid request from src-4.

### Expected outcome

- Each antispam failure, including the replayed response, is refused with a stable code; nothing is stored as an accepted request and no mail is queued.
- At each submission limit the next request is refused, and no request past a limit is accepted or queued.
- The oversized request is refused before processing. The burst is accepted and queued, and outbound mail never exceeds the delivery cap per window.
- src-4's request is accepted and delivered once.

### Evidence to retain

- Per-request source, antispam results, limit counters and dispositions; accepted-request and outbound queue inventories; and the delivery rate per window, as evidence for TM-W-030.
